Assurance at the passing boundary

Section 4.2: Passing boundaries and changes in assurance

A study of 3 observations has 8 possible sequences of successes and failures. Sequences with the same count y have the same predictive probability, so the bundle with count y has mass m3(y), the binomial coefficient times the probability of one sequence. In the example of Section 2, only count 0 meets the width target at both 3 and 4 observations, so A(3) is the probability of FFF. A fourth observation splits each sequence into a failure child and a success child, which take the shares 1 − p3(y) and p3(y) of its probability. Sorting the 16 children by their new count moves only FFFS out of the passing set, so A(4) = A(3) − m3(0) p3(0) = 0.566 − 0.086 = 0.480. With a fixed lower block {0, …, j − 1}, assurance loses only the success share of bundle j − 1.
